The New York-based Human Rights Foundation (HRF) has urged Pakistani authorities to release Dr Mahrang Baloch and provide her with adequate medical care, and called on the international community to speak out about her imprisonment and the wider human-rights situation in Balochistan.

In a dispatch published on Sunday, the foundation described the terrorism charges against the chief organiser of the Baloch Yakjehti Committee (BYC) as "politically motivated" and questioned whether she had received a fair trial.

## The case

According to HRF, Mahrang was arrested in March 2025 during a peaceful sit-in held to protest the deaths of three people at a demonstration the day before. She was then prosecuted under Pakistan's Anti-Terrorism Act.

The foundation said her hearings were held inside the prison, but that she was permitted to take part only by video link — denied, it said, the ability to participate effectively in her own case. Journalists and other independent observers were barred from attending. HRF added that it was concerned by reports her health had worsened in custody, and repeated its call for her release and proper medical treatment.

Marking the International Day of Political Prisoners on 30 October, the foundation said it stood with Mahrang and others jailed for their political views, and urged governments abroad to defend their freedom and basic rights.

## A letter from prison

The dispatch carried extracts from a letter in which Mahrang reflected on her imprisonment and on the path that led her to campaign against enforced disappearances. She recalled visiting her own father in Hudda Jail as a child, carrying Eid cards, and said his loss had shaped her work alongside families searching for missing relatives.

The BYC, she wrote, had chosen peaceful political struggle to confront enforced disappearances, extrajudicial killings, and other abuses — and its members had met arrests, criminal cases, intimidation, and disappearances in return.

"Yet imprisonment has not changed my convictions," she wrote. "I do not consider my own suffering separate from the suffering of my people. My greatest concern has never been simply when I will walk out of this prison. It is when families across Balochistan will no longer have to search for their disappeared loved ones, when young people will be able to express their political beliefs without fear, and when the demand for justice will no longer be treated as a crime."

"Prisons may confine individuals," she added, "but they cannot permanently contain the desire for freedom, dignity, and justice."

## The verdict

On 22 June 2026, an anti-terrorism court in Quetta sentenced Mahrang and fellow BYC leader Sibghatullah Shahji to life imprisonment over the killing of Frontier Corps soldier Shabir Ahmed during a protest in Gwadar. Her legal team rejected the ruling and challenged the fairness of the proceedings, which her sister and legal team member Nadia Baloch called a "faceless trial." Balochistan government officials defended the verdict at the time, maintaining the trial had been fair and the evidence "undeniable."
