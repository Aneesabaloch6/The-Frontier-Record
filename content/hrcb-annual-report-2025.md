The Human Rights Council of Balochistan (HRCB) has documented 1,482 cases of enforced disappearance and 598 killings in Balochistan during 2025, in an annual report describing a severe crackdown on civilians and fundamental freedoms.

![The cover of the Human Rights Council of Balochistan's Annual Report 2025](/static/hrcb-report-2025-cover.jpg){: style="max-width:340px;display:block;margin:1.5rem auto" }

## Key findings at a glance

- **1,482 enforced disappearances** — a 79% increase from 2024.
- **598 killings** — an approximately 25% increase.
- **325 killings** linked to security forces and state-backed death squads.
- **108 minors** among those subjected to enforced disappearance; **29 minors killed**.
- The **Frontier Corps (FC)** linked to 897 disappearances and 195 killings.
- Protesters, journalists, lawyers, and human rights defenders faced systematic legal harassment, arbitrary arrests, and physical violence.

## The wider context

According to HRCB, these violations occurred amid large-scale military operations, communication blackouts, restrictions on freedom of assembly, and the use of counter-terrorism legislation to criminalise legitimate political and humanitarian activity. The figures continue a trajectory the organisation has tracked for years: it recorded 830 enforced disappearances in 2024, and 814 in just the first half of 2025.

The report situates the year's violence within a shrinking civic space — peaceful movements such as the Baloch Yakjehti Committee facing detentions and raids, and an amended Anti-Terrorism Act granting security agencies broader powers of arrest and prolonged detention without trial.

## A note on the figures

HRCB is an independent rights organisation based in Balochistan and Sweden that compiles its data through a network of local volunteers, in a region where Pakistan restricts access for journalists and independent monitors. That closure is part of why such tallies are difficult to verify independently — and why HRCB's documentation carries weight. Pakistani authorities have generally disputed figures of this kind. The organisation's central call is for independent investigation, accountability, and international monitoring.
