The Joint Awami Action Committee (JAAC) has urged residents of Pakistan-administered Kashmir to stock up on two months of rations, and traders to keep their shops open only until 3 October — signalling that a prolonged shutdown, or a territory-wide blockade, may follow a major announcement expected after 4 October.

The call marks a new phase in the confrontation between the JAAC and the authorities. The committee — a grassroots coalition of traders, lawyers, and activists that Islamabad banned in June — led the protest movement that ended in a deadly summer crackdown, and its leaders have signalled that the next phase could include closing the entry points that connect the territory to Pakistan. Mobile data services have already been suspended across parts of the region for more than 100 days.

The administration has signalled a tougher line, invoking last year's violence and recent arrests and stressing the need to enforce the writ of the state.

## Converging pressure

The 4 October date places two mobilisations on the same day. The PTI has postponed its long march on Islamabad — originally set for 27 September — to 4 October, with the march now due to set out from Peshawar. Ahead of both, the capital has again been fortified with shipping containers and road blockades.

The date also carries weight in the territory's own timeline: it was on 4 October 2025 that the government accepted the JAAC's charter of demands under a three-month implementation schedule — an agreement the committee says was never honoured, and whose collapse led to the ban and the crackdown that followed. A year on, the government faces simultaneous pressure in Pakistan-administered Kashmir and on the road to Islamabad.
